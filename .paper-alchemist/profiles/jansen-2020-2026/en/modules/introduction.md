## Rhetorical moves

Establish a practical user-understanding problem, define key constructs, explain why existing representations or evidence are insufficient, then state the study objectives or research questions and scope.

## Organization

Move from organizational or societal context to the particular unresolved issue. Persona papers use definitions and conceptual contrasts; the longitudinal paper links temporal uncertainty to measurable questions. End with a concise study plan rather than another general motivation.

## Language and stance

Explicitly define overloaded terms such as user, customer, audience, personification and analytics. First-person descriptions clarify what the study does; scope-limiting language distinguishes motivation from demonstrated outcomes.

## Evidence and citations

Attach citations to definitions and prior-work gaps. Use the study scope to substantiate the approach, but do not convert a plausible decision-making consequence into an empirically tested finding.

## Cross-lingual transferable patterns

Transfer the narrowing argument and explicit construct definitions; retain target-language sentence structure. Chinese conventions remain unsupported.

## Do

Make the gap specific enough that the proposed analysis can answer it; distinguish conceptual demonstration, longitudinal analysis and controlled comparison.

## Avoid

Avoid claiming all prior work is absent when only a narrow population, setting or evaluation is missing. Do not reuse corpus claims as facts of a new manuscript.

## Generation checklist

Can each research question be traced to the preceding gap and a later analysis? Are the claimed contributions within the actual design?
